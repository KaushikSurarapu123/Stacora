Before you start

How to take your pulse

Smartwatch: read the heart rate number on the screen.

By hand: rest two fingers (not your thumb) on the inside of your wrist, just below the base of your thumb. Count the beats for 15 seconds and multiply by 4.

Stay as still as you can while you measure.

Why Stacora exists

Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS) is a condition where the heart rate jumps when a person stands up. It often starts in the teen years, and it is easy to miss. Doctors use a standing test to look for a sustained rise of 30 bpm or more, or 40 bpm or more for ages 12 to 19. Stacora guides that test at home and turns the readings into a clear page a family can bring to an appointment.

Criteria are based on the consensus definition described in a JAMA review of POTS. Doctors also check blood pressure, which Stacora does not measure.

Questions to ask your doctor

    Stacora is a student project that helps record a home test. It does not diagnose POTS or any condition, and it does not measure blood pressure, which doctors also check. A single home test can be affected by caffeine, hydration, illness, sleep, and how you measured. Please share this page with a doctor and ask what it means for you.
